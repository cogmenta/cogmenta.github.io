01 · Why now

AI today is a personal tool. It never becomes a company asset.

Each person squeezes their know-how into a prompt, gets an output, and takes it or leaves it. Tomorrow they do it again. The firm learns nothing — and the know-how still walks out the door.

Know-howin one person's head
Promptre-typed every time
Black boxhidden reasoning
Outputtake it or leave it
repeat tomorrow

No memory

Yesterday's work doesn't carry into today. Every session starts from zero.

No lineage

No record of why an output came out the way it did — so debugging means re-prompting and hoping.

No company-level gain

Individuals adopt AI individually and partially. The organization's workflow — and its revenue — doesn't move.

02 · Deploy first

We built a small law firm first. Now we deploy inside real ones.

In LawFlow (COLM 2025), we simulated a small law firm — trained law students taking business-formation matters for clients — and recorded the entire decision process end to end: issue spotting, notes, template choice, research, drafting, the client conversation, review.

What we found

Experts are selective. AI is exhaustive.

People plan in layers, follow the branch that matters, and loop back when something changes. LLM agents plan flat and run the plan straight through. The expert's value is in what they don't do — judgment that shows up in the process, never in the output.

03 · Technology

Recursive self-improvement, on-prem.

Frontier labs evolve their models outside the customer. Integrators work inside the customer with a model that never changes. Cogmenta evolves a small local model inside the firm — on one node, with sparse labels, in work nobody can auto-grade.

What evolves — the company's skeleton

  2. Rubricthe definition of "good"outer · weekly
  3. Harnesssearch structure · depthinner · hours
  4. Weightsthe local model itselfouter · gated

  6. Processorder of work · branchinginner + outer
Most self-improving systems stop at prompts and code Cogmenta evolves the whole skeleton

Shallow layers evolve automatically. Deep layers pass a human gate.

Research behind it

LawFlow

04 · On-prem scaling law

The bigger problem isn't intelligence. It's missing context.

Models are smart enough. What they lack is your context — scattered across channels, people, and years, and locked inside the firm. Deployment is how we connect the dots. Connected dots reveal the workflow.

01

Many channels

Email, chat, documents, EHR and practice systems, calls, screens.

02

Many people

Partners, associates, staff — who hands what to whom, and who corrects whom.

03

Many timescales

Minutes of a task, months of a matter, years of the firm's memory.

01

Optimize workflows, company-wide

Tune the workflows the firm actually runs — pitch books, CIMs, claims, charts — across teams, not one person at a time.

02

Discover what nobody wrote down

Surface the gap between the workflow the firm believes it runs and the one it actually runs — and shorter routes no one has documented.

03

Coach experts to work better

Turn a senior's judgment into guidance juniors receive while they work — changing how expertise is practiced, not just recorded.

Across firms: each firm's model stays in its own rack. Only patterns of expertise — never raw data — accumulate upward into vertical and general Expert Foundation Models (XFM) that warm-start the next deployment.
